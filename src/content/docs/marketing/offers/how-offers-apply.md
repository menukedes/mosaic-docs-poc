When more than one offer could apply, Mosaic follows a few simple rules so shoppers always get a fair price and you know what to expect.

## Item offers: the best one wins

Category and Product offers apply to individual items, automatically.

- If several of them cover the same item, the one with the **biggest saving** applies.
- Two offers never combine on the same item.
- Different items in the same cart can each have a different offer.

## Cart offers: the shopper chooses

Order Total, Birthday, Sign-up and Date and Time offers apply to the whole cart. Shoppers choose them in the cart from **Apply Offers & Rewards**.

- Applying one of these **replaces** the item offers already in the cart.
- For Order Total offers, the shopper is asked to confirm first.

:::tip
When you plan an Order Total offer, make sure it's worth more than the item offers a shopper would give up. Otherwise shoppers won't use it.
:::

## Guests and registered shoppers

| Shopper | Offers they can use |
|---|---|
| **Guest** (not signed in) | Category, Product and Order Total offers for **All Users**. |
| **Registered shopper** | Everything a guest can use, plus offers for **Registered Users** and their loyalty tier. If a registered-only offer gives a bigger saving than an **All Users** offer, the better one applies once they sign in. |

Product cards can show a member price when registered shoppers would get a better price than guests. Encourage guests to register or sign in to see it.

## Offers and loyalty rewards

Shoppers can redeem loyalty rewards in the same order as an offer.

## Offers and your POS discounts

If your POS already discounts a product, the storefront shows the best selling price, taking both the POS discount and your Mosaic offers into account. Whether a Mosaic offer can be added on top of a POS discount depends on your POS. Ask the Mosaic support team how it works for your store.

## Examples

**Two offers on one product.** Example Dispensary has "10% off Flower" (Category) and "$5 off this strain" (Product). For an $80 item, 10% is $8 and beats $5, so the shopper gets $8 off. For a $30 item, $5 beats $3, so the shopper gets $5 off.

**Item offers vs. order total.** A shopper's cart has $12 of item discounts. They apply "$10 off orders over $100". The order total offer replaces the item discounts, so they'd save $2 less, and the confirmation lets them change their mind.
